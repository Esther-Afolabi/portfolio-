How I work

The process behind the writing.

Good content is a discipline, not an inspiration. Every piece I write follows the same thinking — the topic changes, the approach doesn't.

Step 01

I start with the reader, not the brief

The brief tells me the topic. The reader tells me the angle. Before I write a word, I want to know who is reading this, what they already believe, and what would actually shift their thinking. That's where the piece begins — not at the keyword.

Step 02

I research until I have something to say

That means primary sources, competitor analysis, and SME interviews where the project allows. I don't start writing until I have a genuine point of view. Thin research produces thin content — and B2B readers, especially buyers close to a decision, can tell the difference immediately.

Step 03

I write for clarity, then edit for precision

My first draft gets the argument down. My edits cut everything that doesn't earn its place. I'm particularly hard on sentences that sound good but say nothing — because those are the ones that quietly erode trust in a brand's voice over time, one article at a time.

Step 04

I optimize for search without writing for algorithms

I understand keyword intent, on-page SEO, and internal linking. I use them as a framework, not a formula. The goal is content that ranks because it deserves to — not content that ranks for three months until Google realizes readers keep bouncing off it.
